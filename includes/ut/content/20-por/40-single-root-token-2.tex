The first example context is \emph{one network split over two chains}.
Both chains use the same \emph{root token}.
There are, naturally, questions to answer,
    like \emph{How are block rewards managed?}
    and \emph{How can the root token be on two blockchains at once?}

Let's consider the following \emph{restricted case} first: the chains (\cL and \cR) have something like a two-way peg between them, ensuring that no coins are ever incorrectly created or destroyed.

In this case, for a single R-block, \autoref{eq:por-conv-work} collapses to
\begin{align}
    \text{ConvWork}_{R\rightarrow L}(w) = \; & \frac{L_d}{L_r} \cdot \frac{R_r}{R_d} \cdot w % \cdot \cancelto{1}{X_{R\rightarrow L}}
        & & % \text{R-hashes }\rightarrow\text{ L-hashes}
        & & \text{Since }X_{R\rightarrow L}=1
        \nonumber
        \\[0.5em]
    \implies \text{ConvWork}_{R\rightarrow L}(R_d) = \; & \frac{L_d}{L_r} \cdot R_r
        & & \text{L-hashes}
        & & w = (R_d \cdot 1) \text{ R-hashes}
        \label{eq:por-conv-work-srt}
        \\[0.5em]
    \implies \text{ConvWork}_{R\rightarrow L}(R_d) \cdot \frac{L_r}{L_d} = \; & R_r
        & & \text{L-coins}
        & & \genfrac{}{}{0pt}{}{\times \text{ L-coins/L-hashes}\qquad\qquad\quad\;\;}{\text{Weight of an R-block in L-coins}}
        % \label{eq:por-conv-work-to-coins-srt}
        \nonumber
\end{align}

So, the weight of an R-block measured in L-coins is exactly equal to the \cR chain's block reward.
This, of course, makes sense.
L-coins are fungible with R-coins in this context, so, naturally, the weight-in-coins of an \cR block is its own block reward.

If we convert like this (treating 1 R-block as worth $R_r \cdot 1$ L-coins) then we'll we need to convert the result to L-hashes \emph{eventually}, but, if we have multiple reflections to process, we can calculate interim values (with units of L-coins) and sum them before that final conversion.
% (since $\nicefrac{L_r}{L_d}$ is a constant for some given moment in time).
It's worth remembering that block-weight is measured \emph{per block}.
Since we're only converting with respect to \emph{a single block} at any one time, the \emph{per block} part of the resulting units \emph{cancels out} --- e.g., \autoref{eq:por-conv-work-srt}.

But where do we get the reflecting chain's block reward?
To start with, we can't let each chain \emph{set} its own reward, that would break the two-way peg.
It also means \emph{the reflecting chain shouldn't be the source of that data}.
We could retrieve it from state (if it's already calculated), but for the sake of this example, let's calculate it in the \textsc{WeightOf} function.

Right now, we don't have enough \emph{context} to know what to do; \emph{what contexts would be useful?}

What \emph{purpose} does a block reward fulfil?
It must be something about \emph{security}, because that's \emph{why} miners are given an incentive to mine (the \emph{how} is via the \emph{block reward}).
If we have multiple chains (with the same root token), why should one chain be more secure than another?
One reason is because \emph{more commerce happens there;} i.e., more of the network relies on \emph{that} chain compared to the other one.

What would make sense, given the context of a commerce-imbalance, to base the block reward on?
A straightforward answer (which can be globally known, too) is \emph{the ratio of root tokens on each chain.}
We can have a \emph{network wide} rule that there is some \emph{network wide inflation rate}, and each chain's block reward is proportional to the \emph{ratio} of coins on that chain relative to the entire supply.
This way, the global inflation rate is predictable, even though the number of coins produced \emph{on each chain} (via block rewards) is variable.
Moreover, with this method we should expect that the \emph{value to the community} of each chain roughly matches the \emph{distribution of the community's activities}, and the \emph{distribution of security}, too.
This method is also consistent with the idea of \emph{equal work for equal reward}.
We can rely on that idea because \emph{equality of work} is a result of the market (for block rewards) formed by participating miners.

If the network uses this rule to determine block reward, then, for either chain $C$, what can we say?
Let: $I$ (coins/s) be the network-wide inflation rate; $G_t$ (coins) be the network-wide root token supply; and $C_t$ (coins) be the number of root tokens on chain $C$. $C_f$ (blocks/s) is the block production frequency of chain $C$.
\begin{align}
    C_r \cdot C_f & = \frac{C_t}{G_t} \cdot I
        & & \frac{\text{C-coins}}{\text{second}}
        & & \text{As set above}
        \nonumber
        \\[0.5em]
    \implies C_r & = \frac{C_t \cdot I}{G_t \cdot C_f}
        & & \frac{\text{C-coins}}{\text{C-block}}
        & & \label{eq:srt-reward}
        \\[0.5em]
    \therefore \frac{R_r}{L_r} =& \; \frac{R_t \cdot I}{G_t \cdot R_f} \cdot \frac{G_t \cdot L_f}{L_t \cdot I}
        & & \frac{\text{L-blocks}}{\text{R-block}}
        & & \text{Via \autoref{eq:srt-reward}}
        \nonumber
        \\[0.0em]
    \frac{R_r}{L_r} =& \; \frac{R_t \cdot L_f}{L_t \cdot R_f} \cdot \cancelto{1}{\frac{G_t \cdot I}{G_t \cdot I}}
        & & \frac{\text{L-blocks}}{\text{R-block}}
        & & \text{by \emph{definition}}
        \label{eq:srt-block-ratios}
\end{align}

Hold up!
Didn't we just go over --- in great detail --- why we can't use block frequencies ($L_f$ and $R_f$) in conversions?
Why is it okay to use them now?

To answer this, let's consider \emph{how} block rewards could be set network-wide so that they're consistent across all chains.
Unlike a chain's difficulty, the block reward needs to be consistent with some global inflation rate.
\autoref{eq:srt-block-ratios} implies that $R_r \cdot R_f \cdot L_t = L_r \cdot L_f \cdot R_t$, so in some cases \emph{we don't need to know the global state, just that the relationship is enforced.}
That said, if $I$ is known across the network (i.e., it is constant or a well known function) then calculating $G_t$ is trivial: $G_t = I \cdot l + G_{t_0}$, where $l$ is the lifetime of the network (in seconds), and $G_{t_0}$ is any initial root token supply that was not created via inflation (e.g., via some token generation event).

Could we maintain consistency across chains via something like a \textbf{Reward Adjustment Algorithm} (RAA)?
If each chain commits to the input values (e.g., via the inclusion of $C_t$ in their block-headers), then an RAA could be evaluated using only the header-chain (similar to the DAA).
This way, if a miner (or a chain) tries to subvert or attack the RAA, it's trivially detectable by reflecting chains.

\defineTermTex{Reward Adjustment Algorithm (RAA)}{
  An algorithm which updates the block reward of each chain in a \emph{network} of chains that share a root token.
  Similar to a DAA, the \emph{output} of an RAA is \emph{context laden}
}

If we use an RAA, then the RAA \emph{output} could become \emph{context laden} (depending on the RAA's input parameters).
In the case of a global inflation rate, \emph{this means that chains would rely on a common meaning of ``seconds''!}
So it is by \emph{construction and definition} that the block frequency introduced in \autoref{eq:srt-reward} becomes part of the convertible context.
Thus, we can \emph{now} include $L_f$ and $R_f$, though we couldn't before.

Note that we also \emph{prove} \autoref{eq:lfrf-ne-rrlr} for this case.
We know that --- when $L_f$ and $R_f$ are in the convertible context --- we can use them via a \emph{scaling factor} that typically $\ne 1$. For example: to convert $\nicefrac{L_f}{R_f}$ to units of L-blocks/R-block, it must be scaled by $\nicefrac{R_t}{L_t}$ (which is contextual and unitless).
We can find a scaling factor \emph{here} (but not generally) because we \emph{included it in a protocol-level relationship} (\autoref{eq:srt-reward}).

This new situation and context are diagrammed in \autoref{fig:daa-raa-conversion}.

Let's use \autoref{eq:srt-block-ratios} to create our next \textsc{WeightOf} algorithm for this context of a \emph{single root token} (SRT) --- \autoref{alg:weightof-ratio}.
\begin{align}
    & \; \frac{R_t \cdot L_f}{L_t \cdot R_f} \cdot L_d
        & & \frac{\text{L-hashes}}{\text{R-block}}
        & & \text{Via \autoref{eq:srt-block-ratios}}
        \nonumber
        \\[0.5em]
    \therefore \text{ConvSRT}_{R\rightarrow L}(b) = & \; \frac{R_t \cdot L_f}{L_t \cdot R_f} \cdot L_d \cdot b
        & & \text{R-blocks } \rightarrow \text{ L-hashes}
        & &
        \label{eq:conv-srt}
\end{align}
